What is electromigration, and what are its effects in semiconductors?
Semiconductor Physics Reliability

Definition: Electromigration is the gradual displacement of metal atoms in a semiconductor's interconnects. It is caused by the momentum transfer from conducting electrons moving through the lattice to the metal ions themselves.

Effects: Over time, this atomic movement degrades the interconnects, causing two main failure modes:

  • Voids: The depletion of metal atoms creates gaps, leading to increased resistance and eventual open circuits.
  • Hillocks: The accumulation of displaced atoms creates extrusions, which can breach dielectric layers and cause short circuits between adjacent wires.
What are the types of CMOS power dissipation, and how can they be reduced?
CMOS Design Low Power VLSI

There are three main components of power dissipation in CMOS circuits:

  1. Dynamic Power: Consumed during transistor switching when charging and discharging parasitic capacitances.
    Reduction Techniques: Voltage scaling (lowering Vdd since power scales quadratically with voltage), clock gating, and reducing physical capacitance.
  2. Short-Circuit Power: Dissipated during the brief moment when both NMOS and PMOS networks are partially ON, creating a direct path from Vdd to Ground.
    Reduction Techniques: Matching the rise and fall times of the input signals to keep the transition window as narrow as possible.
  3. Leakage (Static) Power: Power consumed when the circuit is completely idle, caused by subthreshold leakage and gate oxide tunneling.
    Reduction Techniques: Power gating (shutting off power to idle blocks) and using high-Vt (threshold voltage) transistors in non-critical paths.
How are PMOS and NMOS sized for standard inverters?
Digital Logic Transistor Sizing

To achieve a symmetrical inverter (where the rise time equals the fall time and the switching threshold is exactly Vdd/2), the PMOS and NMOS transistors cannot be identically sized.

Because the mobility of holes (charge carriers in PMOS) in silicon is roughly half to one-third the mobility of electrons (charge carriers in NMOS), the PMOS transistor must be made wider to compensate.

Wp ≈ (2 to 3) × Wn

This sizing ensures that both the pull-up network (PMOS) and the pull-down network (NMOS) provide equal current drive capability.

How is propagation delay estimated in digital circuits?
Timing Analysis

Propagation delay (tp) measures how quickly a circuit responds to input changes. It is officially defined as the time difference between the input crossing 50% of its voltage swing and the output crossing 50% of its voltage swing.

It is typically calculated as the average of two distinct transitions:

  • tpHL: Time for the output to transition from High to Low.
  • tpLH: Time for the output to transition from Low to High.
tp = (tpHL + tpLH) / 2

In initial design phases, engineers use the Elmore Delay model (RC network approximations) or the method of Logical Effort to estimate these delays without running full SPICE simulations.

What is the function of a Phase-Locked Loop (PLL)?
Analog Design Clock Generation

A Phase-Locked Loop (PLL) is an electronic feedback control system designed to generate an output signal whose phase (and consequently frequency) is strictly locked to the phase of an input reference signal.

Core Components:

  • Phase Detector (PD)
  • Low-Pass Filter (LPF)
  • Voltage-Controlled Oscillator (VCO)

Main Functions in Engineering: Clock generation, frequency synthesis (multiplying a slow crystal oscillator clock into a high-speed CPU clock), clock recovery in data communication, and signal jitter reduction.

How do you calculate resistances for attenuator networks?
Circuit Analysis RF Engineering

Attenuators (such as T-pads and Pi-pads) are passive resistor networks used to reduce signal power by a specific amount while keeping the input and output impedances matched to the characteristic impedance (Z0) of the system.

To calculate the resistor values, you first convert the desired attenuation in decibels (dB) into a linear voltage/current ratio factor (K):

K = 10(dB / 20)

For a symmetrical T-Pad network, the resistor equations are:

R1 = R2 = Z0 × [ (K - 1) / (K + 1) ]
R3 (shunt) = Z0 × [ (2 × K) / (K2 - 1) ]
